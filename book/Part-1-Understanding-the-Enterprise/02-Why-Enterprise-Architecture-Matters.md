# What Is an Enterprise?

Many people think an enterprise is simply a company.

In reality, an enterprise is a collection of people, business capabilities, processes, information, applications, technology, and external partners working together toward a common objective.

For SwiftShip Global, that objective is clear:

> Deliver every shipment safely, efficiently, and consistently across the world.

Everything that contributes to that mission forms part of the enterprise.

Enterprise Architecture therefore considers the organization as a whole rather than focusing on individual departments or technologies.

---

# The Invisible Problem

Emma asks:

> "If everyone is working toward the same goal, why do our projects keep failing?"

David, the CIO, replies.

> "Every department solves problems independently."

Sophia, the COO, adds.

> "Operations improve warehouse processes without involving IT."

Olivia, the CFO, says.

> "Finance purchases software without consulting Operations."

Every decision makes sense locally.

Collectively, they create complexity.

---

# Seeing the Enterprise

As the Chief Enterprise Architect, you explain that every major business decision affects multiple layers of the organization.

Business strategy influences business capabilities.

Business capabilities influence business processes.

Business processes require information.

Information is supported by applications.

Applications depend on technology.

Understanding these relationships is the foundation of Enterprise Architecture.

---

# What Is Enterprise Architecture?

Enterprise Architecture is the discipline of understanding, designing, planning, and governing an enterprise so that business strategy, processes, information, applications, and technology work together to achieve common business objectives.

Enterprise Architecture is not primarily about technology.

Its primary purpose is enabling the business to execute its strategy successfully.

---

# Why Enterprise Architecture Matters

Without Enterprise Architecture:

- Departments optimize locally.
- Applications become fragmented.
- Data becomes inconsistent.
- Technology investments duplicate one another.
- Business transformation slows down.

With Enterprise Architecture:

- Business and IT are aligned.
- Investments support strategic objectives.
- Change is coordinated across the enterprise.
- Decision making becomes more consistent.
- Transformation becomes predictable and governed.

---

# Enterprise Architecture at SwiftShip

As Chief Enterprise Architect, your responsibilities include:

- Align business strategy with technology.
- Standardize business capabilities where appropriate.
- Reduce duplication.
- Improve collaboration across regions.
- Support long-term digital transformation.
- Govern architectural decisions.

Your success is measured by enterprise outcomes—not by individual technology implementations.

# Practitioner Scenario

**Scenario**

The COO wants autonomous warehouse robots.

The CIO wants to replace the Warehouse Management System.

The CFO wants to reduce operating costs.

As the Enterprise Architect, what should happen first?

**Recommended Approach**

Understand the enterprise strategy and assess impacts across business capabilities, processes, information, applications, and technology before recommending a solution.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Thinking Enterprise Architecture is only about IT.
- Starting with technology instead of business objectives.
- Optimizing departments independently.
- Ignoring enterprise-wide impacts.

---

# Key Takeaways

- An enterprise is an ecosystem.
- Enterprise Architecture provides a holistic view.
- Business strategy drives architecture.
- Enterprise thinking always comes before solution design.
