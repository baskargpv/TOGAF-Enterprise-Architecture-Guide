# Monday Morning, 8:00 AM

Your phone rings.

> "Congratulations! The Board has approved your appointment as Chief Enterprise Architect at SwiftShip Global. The CEO would like to meet you at 9:00 AM."

You arrive at the company's global headquarters in Singapore.

The reception area is filled with digital screens displaying live shipment data from around the world. Aircraft departures, container ships, warehouse capacity, delivery performance, customs clearance status, and customer satisfaction metrics stream continuously across the displays.

At first glance, everything appears to be running smoothly.

But appearances can be deceptive.

---

# About SwiftShip Global

SwiftShip Global is one of the world's largest logistics companies.

Over the past 25 years, it has grown from a regional courier service into a global logistics powerhouse through rapid expansion and acquisitions.

Today, the company operates:

| Business Metric | Value |
|-----------------|------:|
| Countries | 120 |
| Employees | 180,000+ |
| Warehouses | 450 |
| Distribution Centres | 180 |
| Airports Served | 90 |
| Seaports | 65 |
| Daily Shipments | 35 Million |
| Annual Revenue | USD 78 Billion |

SwiftShip delivers parcels, manages warehouses, transports cargo by air and sea, clears customs, supports global e-commerce, and provides real-time shipment visibility for customers worldwide.

To customers, it appears to be one seamless company.

Internally, however, the reality is very different.

---

# Your First Executive Meeting

The CEO welcomes you.

> "Thank you for joining us."

She pauses for a moment before continuing.

> "Everyone thinks we're a technology company because we have mobile apps, AI, and cloud platforms."

> "But technology isn't our biggest problem."

She projects a dashboard onto the conference room screen.

Several indicators are highlighted in red.

- Customer satisfaction is declining.
- IT costs have increased by 28% over the past three years.
- Delivery performance varies significantly between regions.
- Multiple business units are building similar digital solutions independently.
- New projects are taking longer to deliver.
- Executives receive conflicting reports from different systems.

She turns to you.

> "We don't have one company."

> "We have 120 different ways of doing business."

Silence fills the room.

---

# The Real Situation

As you begin reviewing the organization's landscape, you uncover a series of interconnected problems.

## Different Business Processes

A parcel shipped from Singapore follows a different operational process than one shipped from Germany or Brazil.

Each region has developed its own way of working.

## Different Applications

Several warehouse management systems exist across the company.

Some regions use commercial software.

Others rely on systems developed internally.

Many of them cannot exchange information efficiently.

## Different Data

A single customer may have multiple records across different systems.

Addresses follow different formats.

Product codes differ between regions.

Reports rarely match because the underlying data is inconsistent.

## Different Technology

Some business units operate entirely in the cloud.

Others still rely on aging on-premises infrastructure.

Technology decisions are made independently, with little coordination.

## Different Priorities

Every department believes its projects are the most important.

There is no shared view of strategic priorities.

Projects compete for funding, resources, and executive attention.

---

# The Board's Vision

The Chairperson summarizes the challenge.

> "We don't simply want to modernize our IT systems."

> "We want to transform how SwiftShip operates as a business."

The board outlines five strategic goals:

1. Deliver a consistent customer experience worldwide.
2. Standardize core business capabilities while allowing necessary regional flexibility.
3. Reduce duplication across technology platforms.
4. Accelerate digital transformation and innovation.
5. Build an architecture that supports future growth.

The CEO looks at you.

> "Can Enterprise Architecture help us achieve this?"

You smile.

> "Yes—but only if we begin by understanding the business before designing the technology."

The journey begins.

---

# Your Role

As Chief Enterprise Architect, your responsibilities extend far beyond technology.

You are expected to:

- Align business strategy with technology investments.
- Understand how the organization creates value.
- Define a long-term architectural vision.
- Guide transformation programmes.
- Establish architecture governance.
- Improve collaboration between business and IT.
- Help executives make informed investment decisions.
- Ensure every project contributes to the company's strategic goals.

You are not replacing project managers, solution architects, or software engineers.

Instead, you provide the enterprise-wide perspective that connects their work into a coherent whole.

---

# A Conversation with the CIO

Later that afternoon, the Chief Information Officer joins you.

> **CIO:** "Everyone keeps asking for new systems."

> **You:** "What problem are they trying to solve?"

> **CIO:** "That's exactly the issue. They usually start with the solution before agreeing on the problem."

> **You:** "Then our first task isn't selecting technology."

> **CIO:** "What is it?"

> **You:** "Understanding the enterprise."

The CIO nods.

> "Now I understand why we hired an Enterprise Architect."

# Reflection

Before introducing any framework, pause and consider these questions:

- Why is SwiftShip struggling despite investing heavily in technology?
- Which problems are business problems rather than technology problems?
- If every region chooses its own systems and processes, what challenges might arise as the company continues to grow?
- If you were the Chief Enterprise Architect, where would you begin?

---

# Key Takeaways

- Enterprise Architecture begins with understanding the business, not selecting technology.
- Large organizations often face challenges caused by growth, acquisitions, and inconsistent ways of working.
- The role of the Enterprise Architect is to align strategy, business, data, applications, and technology across the organization.
- SwiftShip Global will serve as the learning environment throughout this book.
